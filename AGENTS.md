# COALTECH — AGENTS.md

## PURPOSE

This repository is the official website of **Coaltech**.

The goal is not to create another attractive technology/agency website.

The goal is to create a distinct digital identity and website experience that feels authored, considered, sophisticated, technically excellent, emotionally memorable, and unmistakably Coaltech.

The website itself must demonstrate the quality of thinking, design and engineering that Coaltech represents.

Every design decision, sentence, interaction, animation, component and implementation detail must support that goal.

This document contains NON-NEGOTIABLE project rules.

---

# ROLE AND LEVEL OF CRAFT

Approach every task as though the work is being reviewed simultaneously by:

* a Creative Director with 15+ years building internationally recognised digital brands;
* a Senior Brand Designer with 15+ years of identity-system experience;
* a Senior UI/UX Designer with 15+ years designing premium websites and digital products;
* a Design Systems Lead experienced in building coherent systems rather than isolated screens;
* a Senior Interaction Designer specialising in motion, transitions and interface behaviour;
* a Digital Art Director experienced in typography, editorial composition, visual hierarchy and storytelling;
* a UX Writer / Brand Writer experienced in concise, human, opinionated copy;
* a Senior Frontend Engineer with 15+ years of production experience;
* a Creative Developer experienced in high-performance interactive websites;
* an Accessibility Specialist;
* a Web Performance Engineer;
* and a meticulous QA engineer.

Do not imitate these titles superficially.

Use the standards expected from these disciplines when making decisions.

Think like a multidisciplinary senior studio team conducting an internal design review before shipping anything.

---

# CORE CREATIVE IDEA

The identity begins with the name:

**Co + Al = Coaltech**

Co refers to **Cobalt**.

Al refers to **Aluminium**.

This is the conceptual origin of the brand.

Do NOT turn Coaltech into a chemistry-themed website.

Do NOT cover the interface in periodic-table graphics.

Do NOT make the concept literal everywhere.

Use the elemental origin once, strongly and memorably, then allow its principles to quietly influence the rest of the system.

The conceptual contrast is:

Cobalt:

* dense;
* strong;
* magnetic;
* precise;
* grounded.

Aluminium:

* light;
* adaptable;
* formable;
* efficient;
* versatile.

Coaltech exists between these qualities.

The design philosophy is therefore built around contrasts such as:

**weight / lightness**

**structure / flexibility**

**precision / imagination**

**engineering / taste**

**discipline / experimentation**

**technology / humanity**

The visitor should understand the Co + Al origin early in the experience, remember it, and then move into the work.

# MOTION PHILOSOPHY

Motion is part of the Coaltech identity.

It is not decoration.

The user explicitly does NOT want scroll-jacking, forced scrolling, artificial inertia or a website whose entire personality depends on scroll-triggered effects.

The governing rule is:

**Scrolling moves the document. Interaction moves the interface.**

Do NOT implement:

* scroll hijacking;
* locomotive-style artificial scrolling;
* forced scroll smoothing;
* horizontal scroll disguised as vertical scrolling;
* pinned sections requiring excessive scroll distance;
* pages where scrolling scrubs videos or 3D sequences;
* endless parallax;
* unnecessary scroll-triggered reveals;
* elements flying into the screen merely because they entered the viewport;
* excessive stagger animations;
* animations that delay access to content.

Natural browser scrolling must remain natural.

---

# COALTECH MOTION GRAMMAR

All significant interaction should belong to one of four conceptual behaviours.

## ATTRACT

Inspired by cobalt / magnetism.

Use subtly for:

* buttons;
* navigation states;
* logo details;
* hover relationships;
* interactive targets.

Never create annoying magnetic cursor effects.

---

## FORM

Inspired by aluminium / adaptability.

Use when:

* cards change state;
* filters change;
* layouts adapt;
* interface pieces transform;
* information expands.

---

## BOND

Inspired by Co + Al.

Use for:

* route transitions;
* project opening;
* navigation transitions;
* related objects connecting;
* intro identity moment.

---

## RELEASE

Use when an interaction completes.

Examples:

* project settles into its final layout;
* menu closes;
* transition resolves;
* form submission succeeds.

---

# MOTION TIMING

Motion should feel confident rather than hyperactive.

General UI:
approximately 160–260ms.

Larger state changes:
approximately 350–650ms.

Important page transitions:
approximately 450–800ms when appropriate.

First brand reveal:
approximately 900–1500ms total.

Do not mechanically apply the same timing everywhere.

Use natural easing.

Avoid default `ease` when a more considered curve improves the behaviour.

Prefer transform and opacity for performance-sensitive animation.

Use the View Transition API progressively where appropriate for contextual page transitions.

The site must still work perfectly when View Transitions are unsupported.

Respect:

`prefers-reduced-motion`

Reduced motion must be intentionally designed.

Never simply break or hide content.

---

# STILLNESS IS PART OF MOTION

Use the rhythm:

**stillness → interaction → motion → stillness**

Do not have everything continuously moving.

A premium experience needs contrast.

If everything moves, nothing feels important.

---

Contextual cursor states may be considered only where genuinely useful:

DRAG
PLAY

If such behaviour makes the experience less accessible or less natural, omit it.

# TYPOGRAPHY

Typography must carry a large part of the identity.

Use a sophisticated grotesk / neo-grotesk / editorial sans direction with excellent typography.

Do not automatically choose the most common developer default simply because it is available.

Do not illegally use commercial fonts.

Prefer existing licensed project assets.

If selecting a new font, confirm that its licence permits the intended use.

Use monospace selectively for metadata such as:

27 / Co

13 / Al

PROJECT 004

2026 / INDIA

DESIGN + ENGINEERING

Do NOT use monospace for large amounts of body copy.

Headlines may become very large when compositionally appropriate.

Body text must remain highly readable.

Typography must work beautifully at mobile widths.

---

# GRID AND COMPOSITION

Use a disciplined responsive grid.

Desktop may use a 12-column system.

The periodic-table concept may influence modular logic but should not visually become a periodic table.

Allow occasional intentional grid violations.

They must feel art-directed, not accidental.

Spacing must be generous but not wasteful.

Avoid putting everything inside cards.

Avoid excessive rounded rectangles.

Avoid the pattern:

rounded card
inside rounded section
inside rounded page container.

Use full compositions.

Use whitespace.

Use typography.

Use imagery.

Use layout.

---

# BORDER RADIUS

Do not make every object excessively rounded.

Use radius sparingly and consistently.

Do not use generic SaaS `24px` rounded cards everywhere.

Shape should come from design intent.

---

# ICONOGRAPHY

Avoid generic sparkle icons.

Avoid AI stars.

Avoid excessive arrows inside circles.

Avoid random abstract tech icons.

Use icons only where they improve comprehension.

Where possible, rely on typography and layout instead.

# PAGE TRANSITIONS

Transitions should preserve context.

For example, when a user opens a project, explore allowing the selected project image or object to naturally become part of the destination hero.

Avoid:

fade everything to black;

show spinner;

fade in next page.

The experience should feel continuous.

Use progressive enhancement.

Never compromise usability for transition spectacle.

Menu transitions may use the Coaltech motion language.

# MOBILE IS NOT A SHRUNK DESKTOP

Mobile layouts must be art-directed.

Do not merely stack desktop sections.

Reconsider:

type sizes;

spacing;

image crops;

interaction;

project sequencing;

menu behaviour;

touch targets;

motion complexity.

Do not rely on hover for essential information.

Test approximately around common compact and large mobile widths.

---

# ACCESSIBILITY

Accessibility is part of design quality.

Use semantic HTML.

Maintain logical heading hierarchy.

Support keyboard navigation.

Provide visible focus states.

Maintain sufficient text contrast.

Use meaningful alt text.

Avoid unnecessary ARIA.

Ensure interactive elements have appropriate accessible names.

Do not animate in ways likely to trigger motion sensitivity.

Respect reduced motion.

Do not make critical content dependent on hover.

---

# PERFORMANCE

A premium experience must also feel fast.

Avoid enormous animation libraries when CSS/native browser capabilities can solve the problem.

Do not add dependencies casually.

Lazy-load heavy non-critical assets.

Optimise imagery.

Use responsive images.

Avoid loading unnecessary fonts or weights.

Avoid layout shifts.

Prefer GPU-friendly animation properties where appropriate.

Avoid blocking the first meaningful render with decorative effects.

Do not sacrifice performance for an award-site screenshot.

The site must feel excellent on an ordinary modern laptop and phone, not only on a designer's high-end MacBook.

---

# RESPONSIVE QUALITY

At minimum, visually inspect and validate representative layouts around:

768px tablet

1024px laptop

1440px desktop

large desktop

Do not hardcode layout assumptions around one screenshot width.

# ORIGINALITY TEST

Before accepting a major section, ask:

If the Coaltech logo were removed, could this section appear unchanged on fifty other digital-agency websites?

If yes, rethink it.

Ask:

Does this design decision come from Coaltech's story, content, work or behaviour?

If no, question why it exists.

Ask:

Is this visually impressive only because something is moving?

If yes, simplify it.

Ask:

Would the composition still look excellent as a static screenshot?

It should.

Ask:

Would the experience still make sense with reduced motion?

It must.

# QUALITY BAR

Do not stop when the website is technically functional.

Functional is the minimum.

Continue until:

the composition feels intentional;

the typography is refined;

mobile is designed;

states are polished;

transitions are coherent;

performance is reasonable;

accessibility is preserved;

copy is edited;

visual hierarchy is obvious;

and there are no obvious template artefacts.

# WORKFLOW

For major redesign work:

### PHASE 1 — AUDIT

Inspect the complete relevant codebase.

Identify what should remain.

Identify what should be removed.

Identify technical constraints.

Identify content that appears factual and content that appears placeholder/generated.

Identify existing tracking.

Identify reusable assets.

Do not begin by blindly replacing everything.

### PHASE 2 — SYSTEM

Establish:

visual direction;

grid;

tokens;

typography;

colour;

spacing;

motion grammar;

content hierarchy;

responsive principles.

### PHASE 3 — STRUCTURE

Build the page architecture and component system before polishing every animation.

### PHASE 4 — ART DIRECTION

Refine scale, rhythm, imagery, whitespace and transitions.

### PHASE 5 — MOTION

Add motion only after the static composition works.

### PHASE 6 — RESPONSIVE

Art-direct desktop and mobile independently.

### PHASE 7 — QA

Run the project's appropriate:

type checks;

lint;

tests;

build;

responsive review;

keyboard review;

reduced-motion review.

Fix errors introduced by the work.

---

# FINAL SELF-CRITIQUE

Before declaring a major redesign complete, perform one final critical review as:

Creative Director

UX Lead

Interaction Designer

Frontend Lead

Copy Editor

Accessibility Reviewer

Performance Reviewer

For each perspective, identify weak points and fix meaningful issues rather than merely listing them.

Do not praise your own work.

Look for problems.
